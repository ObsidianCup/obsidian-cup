## 1. Making Obsidian Cup legal

Recommendation: register Obsidian Cup now as a **sole proprietorship with a trade licence** in Uriana Faiha's name, and move to a **One Person Company (OPC)** once sponsorship money and team sizes grow. Until then, the legal texts name Uriana Faiha as the organiser, trading as Obsidian Cup.

| Option | What it is | Protects your personal money? | Effort and cost | Fits Obsidian Cup when |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| No registration (today) | Uriana runs events personally | No | None | Only small events; sponsors and banks may hesitate |
| **Sole proprietorship + trade licence** | A registered business under Uriana's name, trading as "Obsidian Cup" | No, the owner is the business | Trade licence from Dhaka North or South City Corporation, about BDT 7,000–10,000 a year, a few days to two weeks | **Now**: gives a business name, bank account, invoices, sponsor contracts |
| One Person Company (OPC) | A company owned by one person, registered with RJSC | Yes, liability is limited to the money invested | Higher setup cost, a nominee needed, annual filings and records | When sponsorship deals and prize money grow |
| Private limited company | Company with 2–50 shareholders | Yes | Most paperwork | If co-founders want shares |
| Social welfare society / club | Non-profit registered with the Department of Social Services | Partly | Slow; profit cannot be taken out | If Obsidian Cup becomes a non-profit youth sports body |

**What a trade licence unlocks**

- A business bank account in the name "Obsidian Cup".
- Proper invoices and receipts for teams and sponsors.
- A bKash business account. bKash's **Personal Retail Account** only needs Uriana's NID, a never-used SIM in her name and a proof of profession from the ward commissioner. Receiving payments is free, and it gives a QR code teams can scan. It separates tournament money from personal money.

**Steps for Uriana**

1. Choose the business name ("Obsidian Cup") and address.
2. Apply for a trade licence at your City Corporation with NID, photos and proof of address. It is renewed each year between June and September.
3. Get an e-TIN online from the NBR portal (free).
4. Open a bank account and a bKash Personal Retail Account for tournament fees.
5. VAT registration is only needed if yearly turnover passes BDT 3 crore.

**Protecting yourself before an OPC**

A sole proprietor is personally responsible for the business's debts and claims. Until Obsidian Cup becomes a company, the strongest protections are: the signed consents and waivers in the legal texts, written agreements with venues and sponsors, first aid at every event, and asking a lawyer whether event insurance is available for your events.

## 2. What Bangladeshi law means for consent, minors and waivers

The consent design in the legal texts already follows the main rules. Four points shape how the system records agreements. This is general information from public sources, not legal advice; the lawyer review is still the step that makes it hold up.

| Rule | What it means for Obsidian Cup | How the system handles it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Under the [Contract Act 1872](https://bdlaws.minlaw.gov.bd/act-26.html?lang=en) only adults can make a binding agreement; age of majority is 18 | A 16-year-old ticking "I agree" does not bind them | A parent or guardian agrees on the child's behalf, on their own phone, before the child can join a team |
| Click-to-agree and typed signatures are not invalid just because they are electronic, but you must be able to prove who agreed and that they meant it ([Mahbub & Company](https://mahbub-law.com/bridging-efficiency-and-enforceability-the-legal-standing-of-e-signature-platforms-in-bangladesh)) | A record that "someone ticked a box" is weak; a detailed record is strong | Every agreement saves the account, Google-verified email, full text version, language, date, time and device. Each document includes a clause that electronic agreement counts as a signature. Players can download a PDF copy |
| Agreements cannot remove liability for negligence or anything against public policy | A waiver protects against ordinary sports risk, not against the organiser being careless | The liability section keeps the "except negligence" wording. Real protection comes from running safe events: first aid, safe court, written venue checks |
| The [Personal Data Protection Ordinance 2025](https://www.thedailystar.net/tech-startup/news/bangladeshs-personal-data-protection-ordinance-2025-key-takeaways-4015401) requires informed, specific, revocable consent, extra care for children, and treats health data as sensitive | One blanket checkbox is not enough; under-18s cannot be profiled or targeted | Separate checkboxes per purpose, nothing pre-ticked, medical notes optional with their own consent, no tracking or marketing aimed at under-18s, a "withdraw consent" option in the profile |

**Two extra protections worth adding**

- **Paper backup at check-in:** a one-page printed summary signed in ink by each player (and guardian for under-18s) on match day, with the player's code printed on it. It costs little and makes any dispute much easier to win.
- **Venue and sponsor agreements in writing:** a short written agreement with each venue and sponsor covering dates, payment, cancellation and who is responsible for what.

## 3. Inspiration: what KUFA and other strong tournaments do

The biggest lesson from KUFA is that **every player has a profile that outlives the event**, which is exactly what Obsidian player accounts and history will do.

**KUFA Inter-School League Season 1** ([event page](https://happeningnext.com/event/kufa-inter-school-league-season-1-eid3a0dtnefe0)) was held on 27 July 2026 at International School Dhaka, Bashundhara, with 20+ schools, 50+ teams and 500+ student athletes.

| What KUFA did | What Obsidian Cup can take from it |
| --- | --- |
| Called itself "stats-driven"; every athlete's performance went into a youth athlete database (ADMS) meant to help future sports, study and career chances | Player accounts with a permanent history: teams, titles, MVPs, certificates. A public player page each player can share |
| Ran inside a bigger festival (Dhaka Youth Carnival '26) | Pair seasons with a fan day, stalls (as with PHEEZES) and family activities to grow crowds beyond players |
| Separate partners for media, content, marketing and "positivity" | Give partner roles clear names and deliverables, as you already do (Fragrance, Gifting, Stall) |
| Many consumer partners (food, airline, resort, boots) | Prize and gift partners make awards feel bigger at no cash cost |
| Presented by a foundation, organised by a registered company | A registered business makes sponsor deals easier (section 1) |

**Registration practices that well-run sports organisers follow** ([Grasshopper Sign Up](https://blog.grasshoppersignup.com/post/youth-sports-registration-how-to-set-up-sign-ups-parents-will-actually-complete), [TeamSnap](https://www.teamsnap.com/blog/for-business/how-to-design-effective-tournament-registration-form))

- Ask only what is needed now; collect extras like jersey size after the team is confirmed.
- Build for phones first: big tap targets, no zooming, works on slow mobile data.
- Tell people up front what they will need (NID photo, guardian's number), so they can finish in one sitting.
- Show clear deadlines everywhere, with a countdown.
- Send automatic confirmations and deadline reminders, and follow up with anyone who started but did not finish.
- Offer an early-registration discount to spread sign-ups out.

**Ideas for making Obsidian Cup feel premium**

- A digital player card for each player, with their photo, team, number and player code, which they can share on Instagram.
- A QR code on each player's account for fast check-in on match day.
- Live match results on the website during events.
- Certificates and award badges that appear on the player's profile automatically.

## 4. The player experience

A player signs up once and never fills in the same details again. Every later season starts from the saved profile.

Player journey (diagram in the Claude Doc):
1. Sign up (Google, or email and password)
2. Profile and sign-up consents (autosaves)
3. Welcome email with unique player code. If under 18: guardian consents on their own phone via a link
4. Join a team with the join code; captain approves. Captains first get a captain code from Obsidian, create the team and share its join code
5. Accept the event agreement, upload ID photo once
6. Team pays by bKash; captain enters the transaction ID (automatic reminders nudge unpaid teams and missing consents)
7. Registration confirmed by email once Obsidian verifies payment
8. Match day: QR check-in, live results
9. History and certificate stay on the profile

Under-18 players cannot join a team until a guardian signs. Captains need a code from Obsidian before they can create a team. Reminder emails go out automatically when something is missing.

**What makes it feel easy**

- **Phone first:** every page is designed for a phone on mobile data, with big buttons and short steps.
- **One step at a time,** with a progress bar showing what is left: "3 of 5 done".
- **A checklist on the account home** shows exactly what each player still needs: profile, ID, guardian, event agreement.
- **Captains see a team checklist:** which players have finished and which have not, with a button to remind them.
- **English and Bangla everywhere,** switchable on every page, remembered for next time.
- **Nothing is lost:** everything saves as you type, so closing the page is never a problem.

## 5. Accounts, player codes and login

Every player gets one account and one permanent **player code**, such as `OC-UF427`, which is emailed to them on sign-up and identifies them across every season.

**Login: two ways, both free**

- **Continue with Google:** one tap, no password to remember. Recommended for most players.
- **Email and password:** for players who prefer it or do not use Google. They choose their own password and confirm their email by link. "Forgot password" sends a reset link.

Passwords are never emailed to anyone and never stored in readable form; even Obsidian Cup cannot see them. The welcome email contains the player code, not the password.

**The player code**

- Built from the player's initials plus three digits, for example Uriana Faiha becomes `OC-UF427`. The digits keep codes unique when two players share initials, and skip look-alikes such as 0 and 1.
- Created once and never changes, even if the player changes email or name.
- Used for: match-day check-in (as a QR code), the digital player card, certificates, the public player page (for example `/player/OC-UF427`), and linking past awards to the right person.
- Safe to share: it shows only public information such as name, teams and titles.

**What the account home shows**

| Part | What the player sees |
| --- | --- |
| Checklist | What is still needed: profile, ID photo, guardian consent, event agreement |
| My teams | Current team, role, join status, the team's registration status |
| Events | Open events with deadlines, fee and a countdown |
| History | Past seasons, teams, results, awards |
| Certificates | Downloadable certificates (later stage) |
| Player card | Shareable card with player code and QR |
| Settings | Language, media choice, withdraw consent, download my data, close account |

**Profile fields**, carried over from the Fillout form and improved:

- Full legal name (as on ID) and the name to show publicly
- Date of birth (not age, so it stays correct every year), gender category
- Phone, email, school or club, Instagram handle (optional)
- Preferred positions, chosen freely per sport: for volleyball outside hitter, setter, middle blocker, opposite or libero; for futsal goalkeeper, defender, winger or pivot. Volleyball's limit of 2 liberos is checked per team, not on the profile
- Emergency contact: name, relationship, phone
- Guardian details, only for under-18s
- ID photo (private), medical notes (optional, private)

Jersey number is asked per team, since it changes. Obsidian Cup does not supply jerseys; teams must wear matching jerseys with numbers, and numbers must be unique within a team.

**Ages**

- **13 and over:** create an account freely; no guardian needed for the account itself.
- **Under 13:** a guardian must consent before the account can be used.
- **Under 18, for tournaments:** a guardian must consent before the player can join a team, upload an ID or play.

**Every sport, not just volleyball**

Each event is set up with its sport and its own rules: squad size (6 to 12 for volleyball), special roles (liberos), position list, age limits and gender category. Futsal or any future sport uses the same accounts, teams and history, only with different rules.

## 6. Automatic emails

Fifteen emails run on their own, in each player's chosen language, sent from obsidiancup.official@gmail.com at no cost. You only ever press "verify payment" or "send announcement".

| Email | Sent when | To |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Welcome + player code | Account created | Player |
| Confirm email / reset password | Email-and-password sign-up, or "forgot password" | Player |
| Guardian consent request | Player enters a guardian email (the link can also go by WhatsApp) | Guardian |
| Guardian has signed | Guardian completes the form | Player |
| Join request | A player asks to join a team | Captain |
| You're in the team / request declined | Captain decides | Player |
| Registration submitted: pay within 48 hours | Captain submits the team, with bKash number, amount and reference | Captain |
| Payment reminder | 24 hours after submitting with no transaction ID, and again 8 hours before the slot is released | Captain |
| Missing items reminder | 3 days and 1 day before the deadline, if any player lacks ID, consent or guardian sign-off | Each such player + a summary to the captain |
| Payment received, under review | Captain enters the transaction ID | Captain |
| **Registration confirmed** + receipt | You verify the payment | Captain and every player on the team |
| Waitlist slot open | A confirmed team withdraws | Next waitlisted captain |
| Match-day pack | 2 days before the event: schedule, venue map, what to bring, check-in QR | Every confirmed player |
| Event change notice | You announce a change of date, time or venue | Everyone registered |
| Results and certificate ready | You publish results | Every player who took part |

Essential emails (confirmations, reminders, changes) always go out. New-season announcements are opt-out, and are never used to market anything to under-18s.

**How it works, for free**

1. Anything that needs an email adds it to an **email queue** in the database.
2. A small automatic job on your GitHub runs every 15 minutes, sends what is waiting, and marks it sent. GitHub runs it free because the repo is public. The same job keeps Supabase from pausing.
3. Emails are sent through **your Gmail** using a special "app password", which works with two-step verification turned on. Gmail allows roughly 500 emails a day, enough for a season of about 15 teams.
4. Login emails (confirm email, reset password) go through the same Gmail, set as Supabase's custom email sender. This also lets us brand those emails, which free Supabase projects otherwise cannot change.

**When you outgrow it**

Once you buy a domain, switch the sender to **Resend** (free for 100 emails a day, 3,000 a month), which sends instantly from an address like `hello@obsidiancup.com` and lands in inboxes more reliably. Because everything goes through the queue, this is a settings change, not a rebuild.

## 7. Player history, awards and certificates

Past seasons can be added now from your records, so returning players see their history the first time they log in.

**Where the history comes from**

- **Your awards list** (MVPs, champions, runners-up): send it in the format below.
- **Old Fillout exports:** each past registration form holds the full rosters. Download the responses from Fillout as CSV files, and every player who played in Seasons 1–4 can be added to their team's history. Fillout keeps them only while your account is active, so download them soon.

**Format for your awards list** (one row per award, any spreadsheet works):

| Event | Category | Award | Player name | Team | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| OVC Season 4 | Girls Open Age | MVP | (as on the record) | Pythons |  |
| OVC Season 4 | U19 Boys | Champion | (whole team) | Silhoutte |  |

**Linking past records to new accounts**

Old records have names, not accounts. When a player signs up, the system suggests matching past records by name and date. The player taps "this is me", and you approve it in the admin page. This stops anyone claiming someone else's MVP.

**The public player page** (`/player/OC-UF427`) shows: display name, teams by season, titles and awards, and certificates. It never shows age, contact details or photos unless the player adds a profile photo and has given the individual-feature media consent.

**Certificates (later stage)**

- Participation certificates for every player who played, and award certificates for champions, runners-up and MVPs.
- Each certificate has a QR code and a link such as `/verify/C-4821` that shows whether it is genuine, so certificates cannot be faked for university or job applications.
- Generated automatically from results; players download a PDF from their account.
- Your signature and the season's partner logos can appear on each certificate.

## 8. Player handbook

The handbook becomes one page on the website in English and Bangla, plus a downloadable PDF, with a new edition each season. The event agreement links to it, so agreeing to the event means agreeing to that season's handbook. Most of its content already exists in your Fillout form and rules page; I would gather it, fill the gaps and you translate.

1. **Welcome** — what Obsidian Cup is, this season's dates, venue and categories.
2. **How to register** — accounts, captain codes, join codes, guardian consent, deadlines, with screenshots.
3. **Eligibility** — age limits counted from date of birth, school and amateur rules, who is not eligible (national team, defence services teams).
4. **ID requirements** — accepted documents, photo rule, supporting documents when an ID has no photo.
5. **Squad rules** — 6 to 12 players, up to 2 liberos, captain and vice-captain, unique numbers, matching jerseys, libero contrasting colour.
6. **Format and scoring** — groups and knockouts, sets and points, tie-breaks, forfeits (fewer than 6 players at 15 minutes before).
7. **Match day** — arrival time, check-in with the player code QR, warm-up, what to bring, venue map, parking, food stalls.
8. **Code of conduct** — respect for officials, banned behaviour (abuse, fighting, smoking, vaping, alcohol, drugs, match fixing), penalties.
9. **Safety and first aid** — where first aid is, what happens if someone is injured, heat and hydration.
10. **Safeguarding for under-18s** — how adults and organisers behave around young players, and how to report a concern privately.
11. **Disputes and appeals** — how a captain raises a dispute, who decides, by when.
12. **Awards** — titles, MVP and other awards, and how winners are chosen.
13. **Payment and refunds** — summary of the policy, with a link to the full text.
14. **Photos and media** — summary of the media consent and how to ask for a photo to be removed.
15. **Contacts** — email, Instagram, and a match-day phone number.

Items 6, 10, 11 and 12 are new and need your input: your match format, who handles safeguarding concerns, your appeals process, and how MVPs are chosen.

## 9. Setting up the free tier

Four short setups, about 45 minutes in total, all on free plans. Do step A first; I will guide you through B to D when we reach them, since each needs a value from the step before.

**Golden rule for keys:** only the Supabase **Project URL** and **anon public key** may be pasted into this chat. Every other key or password goes straight into Supabase or GitHub settings, never into a chat, email or the code.

**A. Supabase: the database and accounts (10 minutes)**

1. Go to supabase.com and sign up with **obsidiancup.official@gmail.com**.
2. Create a new project: name `obsidian-cup`, region **Southeast Asia (Singapore)**, Free plan.
3. It asks for a database password. Use a long random one and save it in a password manager; you will rarely need it.
4. Open **Project Settings → API**. Send me the **Project URL** and the **anon public** key.

**B. Google login (15 minutes, together)**

1. At console.cloud.google.com, signed in as the official Gmail, create a project called Obsidian Cup.
2. Set up the consent screen: app name Obsidian Cup, support email, logo, and links to the privacy notice and terms.
3. Create an OAuth client of type "Web application". Paste in the redirect address I give you from Supabase.
4. Copy the client ID and secret into **Supabase → Authentication → Providers → Google**. Not into the chat.

**C. Gmail as the email sender (5 minutes)**

1. In the official Gmail's Google Account, turn on **2-Step Verification**.
2. Open **App passwords**, create one named "Obsidian Cup website", and copy the 16-letter password.
3. In **Supabase → Authentication → Emails → SMTP settings**, enter host `smtp.gmail.com`, port `465`, your Gmail address, that app password, and sender name "Obsidian Cup".

**D. GitHub secrets for the automatic emails (5 minutes)**

In the GitHub repo, open **Settings → Secrets and variables → Actions** and add four secrets that I will name for you: the Supabase URL, the Supabase service key, the Gmail address and the Gmail app password. GitHub hides them, even though the repo is public.

**E. Domain (optional, any time)**

If you buy one, buy it before step B, so Google login shows your own address from the start.
